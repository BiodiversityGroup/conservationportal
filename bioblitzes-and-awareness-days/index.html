Bioblitzes and awareness days

Fixed-window events you can join, or host your own chapter of.

22 resources

/
All categoriesFundingSpecies dataField toolsMappingTraining
  • City Nature Challenge↗

    City-versus-city iNaturalist bioblitz over four days at the end of April. Cities and organizations sign up as organizers

    Sign your city up as an organizer and get four days of concentrated local records

  • Great Southern Bioblitz↗

    The Southern Hemisphere counterpart, four days in spring. You can register your own region

    Register your region for the spring counterpart, so southern seasons are not sampled off-cycle

  • Asia Nature Challenge↗

    Asia-wide iNaturalist bioblitz with an open call for collaborating organizers. Dates have moved between September and October, and no 2026 edition is announced yet

    Offer to co-organize, and confirm dates directly since no 2026 edition is announced yet

  • National Moth Week↗

    Worldwide moth-ing in the last full week of July. Registering your event is the first thing the site asks you to do

    Register a moth night in late July and be listed alongside events worldwide

  • Audubon Christmas Bird Count↗

    December 14 to January 5. The compiler system lets a group run its own count circle, which makes it the strongest host-your-own event here

    Run your own count circle through the compiler system, the easiest way to host a real survey

  • Bat Week↗

    October 24 to 31 every year, with a full organizer toolkit

    Take the organizer toolkit and run a late-October event without designing materials yourself

  • Pollinator Week↗

    Late June, with a toolkit and a map you can add your own activity to

    Add your late-June activity to the map so local people can actually find it

  • World Migratory Bird Day↗

    Two peak days a year, in May and October, with an event registration form and campaign toolkit

    Register an event on either peak day and reuse the ready-made campaign material

  • World Wildlife Day↗

    March 3, the UN and CITES day, with an annual theme and posters in three languages

    Build March 3 outreach around the annual theme, with posters already available in three languages

  • Endangered Species Day↗

    Third Friday in May, with events across the whole month and a promotion toolkit

    Schedule anywhere across May rather than only the third Friday, which the toolkit supports

  • World Wetlands Day↗

    February 2, with a Ramsar materials pack and a global event map

    Pull the Ramsar materials pack for February 2 and add your event to the global map

  • International Vulture Awareness Day↗

    First Saturday of September. Submit your event and it appears on the activities page within a couple of days

    Submit a September event and see it listed within days, fast for an awareness day

  • Save The Frogs Day↗

    April 28 every year, with free event registration

    Register a free amphibian education event on April 28 and be listed globally

  • World Fish Migration Day↗

    Late May, every second year, and it invites you to register your own event

    Register a river event on the biennial date, useful for dam removal and fish passage campaigning

  • WeDigBio↗

    Four-day specimen digitization and transcription blitz, twice a year in April and October. Institutions can register events

    Get a specimen backlog transcribed in four days by registering your collection as a host

  • CWF Great Canadian Bioblitz↗

    Late September observation window with an identification week after

    Use the identification week after the recording window to clean records before they reach GBIF

  • eBird Big Days↗

    One day of worldwide birding, twice a year: the second Saturday of May and again in October. Participation only, there is no local chapter to run

    Join rather than host, and use the single-day snapshot as a global effort comparison

  • Great Backyard Bird Count↗

    Four days in mid-February, from Cornell, Audubon and Birds Canada

    Point beginners at four days in February when you want volume rather than expertise

  • Big Butterfly Count↗

    UK fifteen-minute butterfly counts across three weeks from mid-July

    Ask volunteers for fifteen minutes, the shortest commitment here, across three weeks of summer

  • FrogID Week↗

    Australian frog-call recording blitz in early November, run by the Australian Museum

    Concentrate call recording into November, where a burst of effort yields usable seasonal comparisons

  • Aussie Bird Count↗

    Third week of October

    Ask for twenty minutes in October, with school and council material already prepared for you

  • RSPB Big Garden Birdwatch↗

    One weekend in late January, and one of the largest by participant count

    Reach the largest participant pool here, which matters when the goal is public engagement
