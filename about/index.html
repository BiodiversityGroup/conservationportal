About Conservation Portal

A shelf for the things conservationists already rely on, and cannot easily find.

The tools exist. Grant databases, species registers, camera-trap platforms, ranger toolkits, job boards, reporting systems. What the sector has never had is one place to put them, so most conservationists find them by accident, late, or never.

Conservation Portal holds 558 resources across 41 categories. Every entry was opened and read by a person before it went on the list.

What counts as a resource

Something you can use: a database, a tool, a standard, a guide, a course, a funder, a job board, an event, a service you can hire, a community you can join. Not simply an organization that does conservation work. There are thousands of those, and Connect & Conserve already maps around 8,500 of them.

How it is kept honest

  • A resource that refuses automated requests is checked in a browser before it is called dead, because a 403 is not a death certificate.
  • A live domain is checked for whether it still belongs to the organization named. One former funding atlas is now spam, and a conservation fund's old domain now redirects to a florist.
  • Anything whose newest content is more than two years old is described as dormant rather than presented as current.
  • Where a resource has a real constraint, the entry says so: invitation-only, members only, one country only, paywalled.
  • Where a URL could not be verified, the resource is left off rather than guessed at.

Two rules about ordering

Resources run by The Biodiversity Group, which publishes this directory, are listed first in their category and marked. Saying so is cheaper than being caught not saying it.

Each site appears once per category. A body that publishes a dozen useful pages gets the one link that reaches the rest, so no single organization can fill a page.

Something missing?

Most likely, yes. If you know a resource that belongs here, or find a link that has died since it was checked, send it over.

Conservation Portal is a project of The Biodiversity Group, a 501(c)(3) working on the species most conservation funding overlooks.
